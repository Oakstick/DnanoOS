# Adventures of the Ancient Blades (Rust + wgpu)

The game rebuilt in Rust from the TypeScript/Three.js version one folder up, feature for feature. It uses one codebase for two targets:

| Target | Graphics | How to build | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Windows executable (Steam) | wgpu over Direct3D 12, Vulkan as the fallback | `powershell -File build-windows.ps1` | `dist/windows/Adventures of the Ancient Blades.exe` |
| Web | WebAssembly over WebGPU, WebGL2 as the fallback | `powershell -File build-web.ps1` | `dist/web/` |

wgpu is Rust's implementation of the WebGPU API. The browser build uses the browser's own WebGPU, and the desktop build maps the same calls onto the native APIs. The shaders are WGSL either way.

## Requirements

- Rust stable (`rustup`), plus `rustup target add wasm32-unknown-unknown` for the web build
- `cargo install wasm-bindgen-cli --version 0.2.128` (it must match the `wasm-bindgen` version in `Cargo.lock`)
- On Windows, the Visual Studio C++ build tools (the Windows SDK's `rc.exe` embeds the icon)

## Running

```
cargo run -- --windowed          # debug build in a window
cargo run --release              # borderless fullscreen (F11 or Alt+Enter toggles)
cargo test                       # rules, level generation, movement, audio, input
```

Serve `web/` over http (for example `python -m http.server 5174 --directory web`) after `build-web.ps1`. Browsers will not load wasm from `file://`.

- **Mouse buttons:** left click attacks (sword swing or the held shot), right click drops a bomb. The first left click in play also captures the mouse for aiming.
- **HUD:** health in the top-left corner (portrait, lives, HP bar, weapon, potion effects), score in the top-right corner (gold, nuggets, best). Bombs and the dungeon name sit in the bottom-right.
- **Music tempo:** every 5 nuggets the backing track speeds up by 5% (100 BPM at the start, about 122 BPM at 20 nuggets). Collecting all 25 plays a congratulations fanfare: a brass run, a snare roll, then a ringing chord with sparkles.
- **Menus:** the title screen has Play / Quit Game buttons and the pause screen has Resume / Quit to Title / Quit Game. Use the mouse (hover and click), the arrows or W/S with Enter or Space, or the d-pad or stick with A or Start. The web build leaves out Quit Game.

- **Sword:** each swing has a wind-up, a snapping strike with the hips turning into it and a small lunge, then a follow-through. Swings alternate forehand and backhand, with a sweeping trail.
- **Impact feedback:** micro dust bursts on kills, hits, shots striking walls, landings, footsteps and blasts. Screen shake scales with the impact (a tap for a wall hit, a jolt for a bomb at your feet), and kills get a brief freeze-frame.
- **Loadout panel:** the bottom-middle panel shows the held weapon with ammo pips, shield charges, and each running potion with a bar that drains as it wears off.
- **UI visibility:** larger text, drop shadows on all text, darker bordered panels, a trailing damage chunk on the health bar, and a red screen-edge flash when hurt.
- **Dynamic audio:** every effect varies a little in pitch and volume. Kill streaks climb in pitch, forehand and backhand swishes differ, and footsteps alternate (they squelch in slime). The music layers in extra hats and ghost kicks as enemies close in, and it muffles when health runs low.
- **Juiced rewards:** collected nuggets fly into the HUD counter, which punches in. The score rolls up, floating text pops in, and every five nuggets gets a banner and a chime.
- **Soundtrack:** a Norse war song in D minor at 100 BPM. A war horn opens it over a hurdy-gurdy drone on D and A. Under that sit galloping war drums, stomps and claps, a plucked lyre, a bone-flute melody, a chanting choir in the second half, and "HEY!" shouts at the turnarounds. The first pass builds up layer by layer and every fourth bar ends in a tom roll. `AdventuresOfTheAncientBlades --write-music out.wav [seconds] [intensity]` renders it to a file.
- **Turning on the spot:** standing still, tapping a direction turns Popolon to face it without stepping, and holding it walks. A blocked direction (wall, pit) just turns him, so you can aim a swing, a shot or a jump anywhere.
- **Weight:** Popolon no longer snaps to full speed. Holding a direction accelerates him to the same top speed as before in 0.2 s. Letting go coasts him to rest on the next tile center under friction. Reversing brakes before he turns, and corners bleed a little speed. Hitboxes, `occupied` tiles and collisions are unchanged (`src/logic/hero.rs`, covered by tests).
- **Boss: the Cyclops.** Collecting all 25 nuggets plays the fanfare, then the game fades into the Cyclops' Meadow. It is the same dungeon's outer ring, but the labyrinth in the middle is torn out and replaced with a sunny grassland: grass tufts, flowers, round bushes (some with berries), stone pillars, low brick walls, and a stream crossed by three wooden bridges. The Cyclops is a giant floating eye with horns, bat wings and tentacles. It drops in after the countdown, drifts around keeping its distance, squints and glows to telegraph, then attacks with bolt fans, bolt rings, tracking bolt streams and rolling boulders. Pillars and walls block its shots, and jumping clears them. At two thirds and one third health it grows angrier and faster, a green cross appears, the music steps up, and it starts diving at you. A dive that misses leaves it dazed in the grass, taking double damage. Damage per hit: axe 2, shots 1 (fireball 2), bombs 6. Felling it gives 5000 points and leads on to the next dungeon. Losing a life sends it back into the sky, but it keeps its wounds. In the arena the chase camera turns to keep the Cyclops in frame unless you steer the camera yourself. - **The blacksmith** (Hrolf's forge, four weapon lines of three levels, four shields, eleven potions) has been replaced by the old gatekeeper and the heroes' trees, described above.

- **Sculpted items and objects:** every prop is re-sculpted in voxels at three to four times the old resolution, at the same world size (`src/render/props.rs`). That covers lumpy gold nuggets with glints; green crosses with a bevel and a white plus; a round riveted iron bomb with a rope fuse that sputters sparks; fletched arrows with steel broadheads; banded boomerangs; ragged fireballs; crescent blades; round glass potion flasks half full of their brew, with a cork and twine; faceted shrine gems on carved stone pedestals with glowing rune rings; iron-caged lanterns; and Norse pines for the forest. - **Forest terrain:** dungeon paths are even green grass tiles with only the odd tuft. Their top faces are drawn with parallax occlusion mapping (`grass_shade` in `src/render/shaders.wgsl`, shading mode `SHADE_GRASS`). The fragment shader marches the view ray down into a procedural field of thin, pointed grass blades, about 26 per tile, reaching 7 cm deep. It shades by where the ray lands (dark roots, bright yellow-green tips, each blade its own shade), so the flat tile reads as a lawn with depth. It fades to plain green with distance to avoid shimmer, and it costs nothing on the CPU. Raised ground shows earthen cliffs, and ramps are mossy stone steps. Walls are low mossy mounds edged along every road by a dry-stone fence (two staggered courses and moss-topped cap stones), with ferns and bushes behind it. The woods on top are a mixed forest of pines, oaks and white birches, denser away from the roads. Bombs still blast through, taking the fence, bushes and trees with them. Code: `src/render/view/forest.rs`, trees in `src/render/props.rs`.
- **F1 cheat:** jumps straight to the first Cyclops fight from anywhere, for testing. The FPS counter moved to F3.
- **Recorded soundtrack:** dungeon I plays "The New Adventure" (`assets/music/level1.ogg`), looping, from the countdown on. The title, the shop and the other stages keep the synth tune. The 42 MB studio WAV (48 kHz stereo, 3:39) was encoded to Ogg Vorbis at quality 0.5 (about 148 kbps, 3.9 MB) with Python `soundfile`, writing in 4096-frame blocks because a single large write overflows libsndfile's stack. The file is built into the binary and decoded a packet at a time in the audio callback by `lewton` (pure Rust, so the web build plays it too), resampled to the device rate. It plays in stereo alongside the mono synth effects, ducks under pauses like the synth tune did, and muffles as Ragnar is hurt. It does not speed up every five nuggets; only the synth tune does. Code: `src/audio/track.rs`. To add a track, encode it the same way, add a `Soundtrack` variant and pick it in `Game::enter`.

The classic dungeon is mirrored right and down again so floor levels meet across the seams, and passages are opened through the shared walls. The billows are lit by the moon from their own slope, in smooth quintic noise so the lighting shows no seams. A rank thins away as the camera comes up to it, so the view from near an edge stays clear, and the ranks between a far camera and the island (the map view) are left out.
- **Better shadows, lighting and effects:** the moon's shadow map is now two cascades in one array: a fine one (20 x 20 units, about 200 texels a unit) over the ground ahead of Ragnar, stepped a whole texel at a time so its edges never crawl, and the whole-level one. Both are filtered as soft shadows that harden where an object meets the ground (a blocker search sets how far a 16-tap Vogel disc spreads), with a much smaller bias, so shadows no longer float off their casters. Moonlight streams past trees and smoke as light shafts when the moon is on screen (`fs_rays`, added into the glow). Lanterns and candles near Ragnar wear soft halos in the damp air, five candles (was three) cast light, fireflies flash in their own rhythms instead of glowing steadily, three will-o'-the-wisps wander over the churchyard trailing sparks and throwing a pale green light, braziers and fire bowls throw up embers, and characters and statues catch a cool rim of moonlight. Point lights out of reach are skipped in every shader, which paid for all of this: a 1080p frame costs about what it did before.
- **More mist and smoke:** ground mist is thicker (three sheets everywhere, four over water and graves), and smoke curls up from the gate's braziers and from every candle in the garden.
- **The Cyclops' Meadow at dusk:** the boss arena gets the same treatment. The sky is a new dusk skybox: flame at the horizon through rose and violet to deep blue, a low sun with a wide glow, streaky clouds lit gold on the sun's side and the first stars overhead (`dusk_sky`). The arena floats on a mauve cloud sea, its lawn has the parallax grass, and its stream runs in the stone channel with the reflective water, lilies and reeds under stone bridges. Fire bowls smoke on the stone pillars, and mist lies over it all.
- **Dungeon I, an English landscape garden:** the first dungeon is dressed as a night-time landscape garden, planned from its fixed maze by `src/logic/garden.rs`. Its water now runs from the Krampus fountain in four channels (see above) in stone-edged beds with lily pads and reeds; wherever a channel crosses a path, the crossing becomes a balustraded stone bridge (stepped where the path climbs a ramp). The boss meadow's stream still rises from a rocky grotto. The garden also has a churchyard behind wrought-iron railings (leaning headstones, crosses, obelisks, a dead tree, a tomb and grave lanterns), walled flower gardens and parterres inside clipped box hedges (roses, lavender and white, yellow and blue blooms with topiary and urns), two round classical temples with green copper domes, and six wayside shrines and urns. Candles in the shrines, temples and graves light their surroundings. The water, the fountain, the churchyard and the temples cannot be bombed. **Slimes cannot cross bridges:** the channels split the garden, and the slimes stay in their quarter. The water is drawn each frame with its own shader (`SHADE_WATER`): rippled, mirroring the starry sky and the moon, glinting with lantern light, with falling cascades and foam. Code: `src/render/view/garden.rs`.
- **Night mist:** thin drifting sheets of ground mist lie over every forest level, thicker over water and graves. They thin out right in front of the camera and are warmed by nearby lights.
- **Slime trails:** slimes leave glossy splats of goo with droplets flung around them, smeared into one continuous track from tile to tile. Bubbles swell and pop in them, and they shrink away as they dry.
- **Night sky and cloud sea:** the forest levels sit under a black sky with three layers of twinkling stars, a faint milky band and a full moon (a full-screen sky pass, `fs_sky`). The dungeon floats on a sea of drifting dark clouds: stacked procedural mist layers (`SHADE_CLOUD`) that bank up against the level's sides and thin out away from it.
- **Lighting and post-processing (stage 1):** each frame draws a 4096² shadow map from the moon (or the meadow's sun) over the whole level, sampled with a 16-tap filter for soft edges. The scene pass writes color plus world normals and view depth. From that buffer come contact shading (SSAO, at half resolution with a depth-aware blur), a five-level bloom chain for glow, and a composite with background depth of field (in focus on Ragnar), gentle grading and a vignette. The HUD is drawn afterwards, sharp. Code: `src/render/gpu.rs`, `src/render/post.wgsl`.
- **Detailed Ragnar:** the hero is sculpted at twice the old resolution (30x46x18, `ragnar_hd` in `src/render/characters.rs`). It has a riveted spangenhelm with bronze strips and ivory horns, a braided fur beard with gold rings, a fur mantle, a woven tunic with embroidered hem, a stitched belt with gold buckle and pouch, laced bracers, cross-gartered trousers, fur-cuffed boots, and all axes and shields rebuilt. Voxels carry materials in the top byte of their color (metal, fur, cloth, leather, skin, ivory, wood, gold). The shader gives each face its own procedural detail and shine (`voxel_surface`): hammered dents, woven threads, fur strands, wood grain and polished highlights, faded out at a distance. The HUD portrait keeps the old pixel model.

## Layout

```
src/logic/     rules: grid, level generation, hero, enemies, items, projectiles, systems
src/game.rs    screens, the round, and the rules that tie entities together
src/render/    view.rs (scene -> instances + lights), gpu.rs (wgpu), shaders.wgsl, post.wgsl, hud.rs, overlay.rs, voxels.rs
src/audio/     software synth, lo-fi music sequencer, sound effects
src/input.rs   keyboard, mouse, touch and gamepad mapped to actions
src/app.rs     winit window, event loop, fixed 120 Hz step
src/capture.rs headless screenshots: --capture out.png --time 6 --script "0.1:confirm,0.3:pause,0.6:confirm,3:+up,4:bomb" [--iso] [--closeup DEG] [--view x:y:z:angle:dist[:rise]] [--bench N]; beat:N:T jumps into Scene I
steam/         SteamPipe build scripts and the release checklist
```

The dungeon generator uses the same seeded RNG and hash as the TypeScript version, and a test checks that the generated layout matches the web build tile for tile. Lighting follows Three.js (Lambert, hemisphere, physically based point-light falloff, ACES filmic, fog after sRGB encode). Sampled pixels match the Three.js build to within one or two levels.
